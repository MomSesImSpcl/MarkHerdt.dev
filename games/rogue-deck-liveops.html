Rogue Deck: LiveOps & Economy

How I would run Rogue Deck as a free-to-play live game: what players collect, how they earn it, what keeps them coming back each day, week and season, and how the economy holds together.

This is a design plan for features on my backlog, not a shipped system. Prices and reward values are assumptions, tuned in an editable economy model. Rarity weights, effect counts and match length come from the current game.
ModelFree-to-play, cosmetic and collection focused

Design pillars

1. Effects are earned fast, art is the long chase

A regular free player owns 84% of all effects after two months. The long-term goal is collecting art styles, which never change how a card plays.

2. Every mode has a fair option

Random and Draft matches ignore your collection completely. Only Normal mode uses your own decks.

3. Money speeds things up, it doesn't unlock anything

Every card and effect can be earned for free. Paid offers save time or add cosmetics.

4. Give players a reason for every time frame

Something to do in one match, one session, one week and one season, so there's always a next goal.

Collection

Every card has illustrations in four art styles: Default, Holo, Full Art and Alt Art. This applies to playing cards and Rogue cards alike. A card in a booster pack has a random effect and a random art style. The art style is purely cosmetic and never changes the effect.

Full Art1%1 in 20 packsRare art-upgrade token (events)
Alt Art0.1%1 in 200 packsPacks only, plus new event art over time

Effect rarity uses the same 8 / 4 / 2 / 1 weights as the in-match Rogue offers, so a pack averages one Legendary every three packs. Each pack contains 1 Rogue card + 4 playing cards.

Crafting keeps packs relevant

Duplicates can be dismantled into card fragments, and any card can be crafted. Crafted cards always get the Default art. Players can complete a deck faster, but rare art only comes from packs and events.

Dismantling a Holo, Full Art or Alt Art card returns 2×, 4× or 8× the materials, so rare duplicates still feel valuable. Nerf refunds: if a card is nerfed, dismantling it returns its full craft cost instead.

Collection milestones

  • Own X unique cards (repeating tiers)
  • Own every effect in a category (Scoring, Tempo, Hand…)
  • Own every rarity of an effect, e.g. Score Multiplier ×2 to ×5
  • Own every art style of one card (Alt Art and event art excluded)

  • Gold comes from matches and quests, and buys packs (150 gold), cosmetics and quest rerolls. Match gold is capped at 30 per day (about 4 matches). That stops bots from farming and keeps quests and gems relevant.
  • Gems are bought with real money and buy packs (100 gems), cosmetics and bundles. They are never needed to get a card.
  • Card fragments (crafting materials) come only from dismantling duplicates and from rewards, and are spent only on crafting.
  • Event tokens exist only during an event and buy from that event's shop, including art-upgrade tokens.

Collection progress

A Monte Carlo simulation (300 runs per profile) opens these packs month by month:

All 61 effects owned
All 312 artworks owned
  • Effects saturate quickly. Every profile except Casual owns 90% or more of all effects by month 3. Competitive power evens out early, which supports pillar 3.
  • Art keeps going. After six months, even the paying profile owns only 55% of artworks. The long tail is mostly Full Art and Alt Art, and it gets longer with every new art set. That makes it the long-term collecting goal.
  • Paying speeds things up but doesn't create a gap. At month 6 the paid profile owns 100% of effects versus 98% for the same player on the free path.

Season calendar

One season is four weeks. The events are designed to feed each other instead of competing for the same session:

Week 1Week 2Week 3Week 4Battle passMonthly quests + login trackWeekly rule modifierWeekend Draft cupRotating booster packSpecial event + reward trackEvent bundle sale
  • Weekly rule modifiers are limited-time rule changes that reuse existing systems, so they're cheap to produce. Examples: half-price Rogue cards, every player starts with one Legendary, or same-rank cards also score (the idea I dropped for the base game works fine as a one-week twist).
  • The weekly quests point to the modifier ("Win 3 matches this week"), and the rotating booster pack is themed to match. A multiplier pack runs alongside the half-price Rogue week, for example.
  • Weekend Draft cups award event tokens. During the special event, those tokens also count toward the event reward track, which hands out art-upgrade tokens. The tokens feed the collection milestones.
  • The event bundle sale runs at the start of the special event, when engagement is highest. Seasonal sales (Christmas, anniversary) add better gem rates.

Retention systems

Onboarding: the first hours

  • New players get a complete starter deck. It isn't the best deck, but it's a good one, so the first matches are fun and winnable.
  • One-time quests pay out generously and introduce each system: open a pack, craft a card, play Draft, change a card back, visit the shop. The shop quest shows both payment options, gold and gems, without forcing a purchase.
  • The aim is an early run of fun matches and good cards. Players who enjoy their first hours and feel they own something are more likely to come back on day 2.

Daily, weekly and monthly quests

  • More quests than required: 5 daily (3 needed), 6 weekly (4 needed), 8 monthly (5 needed). Players pick what fits how they like to play. Finishing all of them gives a small bonus.
  • Reward per quest, then a bigger finish: weekly and monthly quests pay something for each quest, so progress is never "all or nothing".
  • Rerolls are specified in full in the feature spec below.
  • One daily quest can target a known design problem. "Win a match with 3 Common Rogue effects" directly tests the underbought-Commons finding from the playtest logs.

Logging in and coming back

  • Daily free pack for a very easy objective (play one match).
  • Monthly login track with no streak reset: rewards grow with the number of days logged in, so missing a day never wipes out progress.
  • Comeback rewards: after 7 days away, a welcome-back mission plus 1 pack. After 30 days, a short mission chain plus 3 packs.
  • Account milestones with slowly growing rewards, including loyalty cosmetics that can't be bought.

Guardrails

  • All drop rates are shown in the shop, and rare pulls are highlighted during pack opening.
  • Every card and effect can be earned for free. Random and Draft ignore the collection entirely.
  • Crafted cards stay Default art, so crafting never replaces packs.
  • Nerfed cards can be dismantled for their full craft cost.
  • A pity timer guarantees a Full Art or better within a set number of packs (planned, not yet in the model).

Feature spec: Quests & rerolls

A handoff-ready spec for one system, written for engineering, UI and QA.

Goals

  • Give every session a short, clear set of goals.
  • Let players choose quests that match how they like to play.
  • Steer players toward modes, mechanics and the weekly modifier.

Non-goals

  • Quests aren't the main source of power.
  • No quest should require spending real money.

Rules

Paid reroll (first)10 gold25 gold50 gold
  • Each paid reroll in the same period doubles the price. A paid reroll costs one to two quests' worth of gold, so it's a convenience, not a way to farm.
  • A reroll never brings back a quest that was already offered, unless every quest in the pool has been seen.
  • Completed or claimed quests can't be rerolled. Rerolling a quest that's partly done resets its progress, and the dialog says so.
  • Reset times: daily at 04:00 local time, weekly on Monday, monthly on the 1st. Unclaimed rewards are claimed automatically at reset.
  • If a quest becomes impossible (e.g., its event mode ended), it's replaced for free.

QA acceptance criteria

  • The first reroll in each category is free; the next costs the base price and then doubles.
  • A reroll never shows a previously offered quest while unseen quests remain in the pool.
  • Progress, rewards and reroll counts survive app restarts and are validated on the server.
  • Completing more quests than required grants the bonus exactly once.
