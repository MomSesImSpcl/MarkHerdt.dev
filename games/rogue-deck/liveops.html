Rogue Deck: LiveOps & Economy

How I'm planning on running Rogue Deck as a free-to-play live game: what players collect, how they earn it, what keeps them coming back each day, week and season, and how the economy holds together.

This is a design plan for features on my backlog, not a shipped system. Prices and reward values are assumptions, tuned in an editable economy model. Rarity weights, effect counts and match length come from the current game.

Design pillars

1. Effects are earned quicker than art

A regular free player owns 84% of all effects after about two months (With the curent effect catalog). The long-term goal is collecting art styles, which never change how a card plays.

2. Fair modes alongside collection play

Normal mode uses your own decks. Random and Draft give every player the same starting point, no matter what they own.

3. Money speeds things up, it doesn't unlock everything

Every card and effect can be earned for free. Paid offers save time or add cosmetics.

4. Give players a reason for every time frame

Something to do in one match, one session, one week and one season, so there's always a next goal.

Collection

Every card has illustrations in four art styles: Default, Holo, Full Art and Alt Art. This applies to playing cards and Rogue cards alike. A card in a booster pack has a random effect and a random art style. The art style is purely cosmetic and never changes the effect.

Full Art1%1 in 20 packsRare art-upgrade token (events)
Alt Art0.1%1 in 200 packsPacks only, plus new event art over time

Effect rarity uses the same 8 / 4 / 2 / 1 weights as the in-match Rogue offers, so a pack averages one Legendary every three packs. Each pack contains 1 Rogue card + 4 playing cards.

Crafting keeps packs relevant

Duplicates can be dismantled into card fragments, and any card can be crafted. Crafted cards always get the Default art. Rare art only comes from packs and events.

The goal of crafting: a player can complete a deck relatively quickly, by crafting the last missing cards instead of waiting for lucky pulls. Building several competitive decks still takes time, so packs, events and long-term play stay valuable.

The fragments returned depend only on the effect's rarity, never on the art style, so rare art is something you keep, not something you cash in. Nerf refunds: if a card is nerfed, dismantling it returns its full craft cost instead.

Collection milestones

  • Own X unique cards (repeating tiers)
  • Own every effect in a category (Scoring, Tempo, Hand…)
  • Own every rarity of an effect, e.g. Score Multiplier ×2 to ×5
  • Own every art style of one card (Alt Art and event art excluded)

  • Gold comes from matches and quests, and buys packs (150 gold), cosmetics and quest rerolls. Gold that can be earned fomr matches has a daily limit. That stops bots from farming and keeps quests and gems relevant.
  • Gems are bought with real money and buy packs (100 gems), cosmetics and bundles. They are never needed to get a card.
  • Card fragments (crafting materials) come only from dismantling duplicates and from rewards, and are spent only on crafting.
  • Tokens are general-purpose rewards that drop during events and from the battle pass: a cosmetic token can be exchanged for any cosmetic in the shop, an art-upgrade token upgrades one card's art style. There is no event-specific currency.

Collection progress

A Monte Carlo simulation (300 runs per profile) opens these packs month by month:

All 61 effects owned
All 312 artworks owned
  • Effects saturate quickly. Every profile except Casual owns 90% or more of all effects by month 3. Competitive power evens out early, which supports pillar 3.
  • Art keeps going. After six months, even the Hardcore profile owns only 54% of artworks. The long tail is mostly Full Art and Alt Art, and it gets longer with every new art set. That makes it the long-term collecting goal.
  • Paying speeds things up but doesn't create a gap. The battle pass lifts an Engaged player from 90% to 93% of effects after two months; by month 4 both are within a few percent of a full set.

These numbers use the effects in the game today. New effects will be added during development and through LiveOps, so the percentages drop with every release and the collection keeps growing.

A deck for Normal mode has two parts: a 52-card main deck and a 26-card Rogue deck. Rarity limits keep every deck within the balance the game was tuned for:

Season calendar

One season is four weeks. The events are designed to feed each other instead of competing for the same session:

Week 1Week 2Week 3Week 4Battle passMonthly quests + login trackWeekly rule modifierWeekly booster rotationWeekend Draft cupSpecial event + reward track (2 weekends)Event bundle sale
  • Weekly rule modifiers change a few rules or card effects for one week, so players are encouraged to try new deck builds. They reuse existing systems, so they're cheap to produce. Examples: half-price Rogue cards, every player starts with one Legendary, or same-rank cards also score, etc.
    • Effects are only boosted, never nerfed. A temporary buff feels like a bonus; a temporary nerf feels like losing something you own.
    • Only a few effects change per week, so updating a deck takes minutes, not an evening.
    • Changes start every Monday and stay fixed for the whole week. The next week's changes are announced over the weekend, so players can prepare their decks in advance.
    • They apply to all modes, including ranked.
    • The changes double as a live test for planned balance changes, with real player data before anything becomes permanent.
  • The weekly booster pack runs all month and is themed to that week's boosted effects: in a week where Score Multiplier is boosted, the pack has a higher chance of multiplier effects. The weekly quests point the same way.
  • Weekend Draft cups reward tokens. The special event runs for 9 days across two weekends, so players who mostly play on weekends don't miss it, and its reward track hands out cosmetic and art-upgrade tokens that feed the collection milestones.
  • The event bundle sale runs on the event's first weekend, when engagement is highest. Seasonal sales (Christmas, anniversary) add better gem rates.

Retention systems

Onboarding: the first hours

  • New players get a complete starter deck. It isn't the best deck, but it's good enough, so the first matches are fun and winnable.
  • One-time quests pay out generously and introduce each system: open a pack, craft a card, play Draft, change a card back, visit the shop. The shop quest shows both payment options, gold and gems, without forcing a purchase.
  • The aim is an early run of fun matches and good cards. Players who enjoy their first hours and feel they own something are more likely to come back on day 2.

Daily, weekly and monthly quests

  • More quests than required: 5 daily (3 needed), 6 weekly (4 needed), 8 monthly (5 needed). Players pick what fits how they like to play. Finishing all of them gives a small bonus.
  • Reward per quest, then a bigger finish: weekly and monthly quests pay something for each quest, so progress is never "all or nothing".
  • Rerolls: one free reroll per category, after that a small fee that rises with each reroll. A reroll never brings back a quest that was already offered.
  • One daily quest can target a known design problem. "Win a match with 3 Common Rogue effects" directly tests the underbought-Commons finding from the playtest logs.

Logging in and coming back

  • Daily free pack for a very easy objective (play one match).
  • Monthly login track with no streak reset: rewards grow with the number of days logged in, so missing a day never wipes out progress.
  • Comeback rewards: after 7 days away, a welcome-back mission plus 1 pack. After 30 days, a short mission chain plus 3 packs.
  • Account milestones with slowly growing rewards, including loyalty cosmetics that can't be bought.

Battle pass

TierPriceContents
Premium$9.99About $30 of value: gems, packs, card fragments, cosmetic tokens (choose your own cosmetic) and season-specific cosmetics
Epic$19.99Premium + instant 15-level jump + exclusive cosmetic

  • 50 levels, 1,000 XP each. Every level costs the same. After level 50, rewards repeat, so engaged players keep earning.
  • Weekly challenges are the main XP source: 5 challenges per week, 2,000 XP each (10,000 XP per week), doable in about 5 matches, e.g. "Play 3 matches", "Win a Draft match" or "Play under this week's modifier". Unfinished challenges carry over until the season ends, so a missed week can be caught up.
  • XP per match comes on top: PvP win 500, PvP loss 250 (surrendering earns nothing), plus 10 XP per active minute. PvE matches earn less XP depending on difficulty. An average 15-minute match is worth about 525 XP.
  • Only bought with money, not gems. Upgrading to a higher tier costs only the difference.
  • Season leaderboard by battle pass level, with achievements for level milestones (e.g. level 100 and 150) and a cosmetic for the top 1% of the season.

Why weekly challenges: if XP came only from matches, any setting that lets a Casual player finish would let everyone else finish within days. Weekly challenges give every player the same fixed amount of XP for a few sessions per week, so even a Casual player can finish the $9.99 pass by playing about 5 matches a week, while Regular players still need most of the season. Epic and Legendary save real time, and Engaged players keep going with repeating rewards, the leaderboard and level milestones. If paying players can't finish what they bought, they're unlikely to buy the next pass.

Guardrails

  • All drop rates are shown in the shop, and rare pulls are highlighted during pack opening.
  • Every card and effect can be earned for free. Random and Draft ignore the collection entirely.
  • Crafted cards stay Default art, so crafting never replaces packs.
  • Nerfed cards can be dismantled for their full craft cost.
  • A pity system guarantees a Legendary within a set number of packs.
