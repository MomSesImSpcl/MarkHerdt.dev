Rogue Deck: LiveOps & Economy

How I'm planning on running Rogue Deck as a free-to-play live game: what players collect, how they earn it, what keeps them coming back each day, week and season, and how the economy holds together.

This is a design plan for features on my backlog, not a shipped system. Prices and reward values are assumptions, tuned in an editable economy model. Rarity weights, effect counts and match length come from the current game.
ModelFree-to-play, cosmetic and collection focused

Design pillars

1. Effects are earned quicker than art

A regular free player owns 86% of all effects after about two months (with the current effect catalog). The long-term goal is collecting art styles, which never change how a card plays.

2. Fair modes alongside collection play

Normal mode uses your own decks. Random and Draft give every player the same starting point, no matter what they own.

3. Money speeds things up, it doesn't unlock everything

Every card and effect can be earned for free. Paid offers save time or add cosmetics.

4. Give players a reason for every time frame

Something to do in one match, one session, one week and one season, so there's always a next goal.

Collection

Every card has illustrations in four art styles: Default, Holo, Full Art and Alt Art. This applies to playing cards and Rogue cards alike. A card in a booster pack has a random effect and a random art style. The art style is purely cosmetic and never changes the effect.

Art styleChance per cardAverage (5 cards per pack)Where else to get it
Default90%9 in 2 packsCrafting, starter decks
Holo8.9%9 in 20 packsArt-upgrade token (events)
Full Art1%1 in 20 packsRare art-upgrade token (events)
Alt Art0.1%1 in 200 packsPacks only, plus new event art over time

Each pack contains 1 Rogue card + 4 playing cards. Effect rarity uses the same 8 / 4 / 2 / 1 weights as the in-match Rogue offers, so a pack averages one Legendary every three packs.

Average = chance per card × 5 cards, e.g. Default: 90% × 5 = 4.5 cards per pack (9 in 2 packs). Legendary: the weights add up to 8 + 4 + 2 + 1 = 15, so each card is Legendary with a chance of 1 in 15; with 5 cards per pack that is 5 in 15, or 1 in 3 packs.

Crafting keeps packs relevant

Duplicates can be dismantled into card fragments, and any card can be crafted. Crafted cards always get the Default art. Rare art only comes from packs and events.

The goal of crafting: a player can complete a deck relatively quickly, by crafting the last missing cards instead of waiting for lucky pulls. Building several competitive decks still takes time, so packs, events and long-term play stay valuable.

The fragments returned depend only on the effect's rarity, never on the art style, so rare art is something you keep, not something you cash in. Nerf refunds: if a card is nerfed, dismantling it returns its full craft cost instead.

Collection milestones

  • Own X unique cards (repeating tiers)
  • Own every effect in a category (Scoring, Tempo, Hand…)
  • Own every rarity of an effect, e.g. Score Multiplier ×2 to ×5
  • Own every art style of one card (Alt Art and event art excluded)

  • Gold comes from matches and quests, and buys packs (150 gold), cosmetics and quest rerolls. Gold that can be earned from matches has a daily limit. That stops bots from farming and keeps quests and gems relevant.
  • Gems are bought with real money and buy packs (100 gems), cosmetics and bundles. They are never needed to get a card.
  • Card fragments (crafting materials) come only from dismantling duplicates and from rewards, and are spent only on crafting.
  • Tokens are general-purpose rewards that drop during events and from the battle pass: a cosmetic token can be exchanged for any cosmetic in the shop, an art-upgrade token upgrades one card's art style. There is no event-specific currency.

What the model shows: the daily cap does its job. The hardcore profile plays 2.3× as many hours as the engaged one (90 vs. 39) but gets only 13% more free packs (55.8 vs. 49.3). Logging in regularly is rewarded much more than grinding, which is better for player health.

Collection progress

A Monte Carlo simulation (300 runs per profile) opens these packs month by month. The code is on GitHub, so the results can be reproduced with any assumptions:

All 61 effects owned
All 312 artworks owned
  • Effects saturate quickly. Every profile except Casual owns 90% or more of all effects by month 3. Competitive power evens out early, which supports pillar 3.
  • Art keeps going. After six months, even the Hardcore profile owns only 54% of artworks. The long tail is mostly Full Art and Alt Art, and it gets longer with every new art set. That makes it the long-term collecting goal.
  • Paying speeds things up but doesn't create a gap. Without spending, a Regular player owns 86% of effects after two months (34.7 free packs per month). Spending lifts an Engaged player from 91% to 94% of effects after two months; by month 4 both are within a few percent of a full set.

These numbers use the effects in the game today. New effects will be added during development and through LiveOps, so the percentages drop with every release and the collection keeps growing.

Deck building

A deck for Normal mode has two parts: a 52-card main deck and a 26-card Rogue deck. Rarity limits keep every deck within the balance the game was tuned for:

The Rogue deck can't contain duplicates. Each turn offers 3 cards, drawn from your own 26-card Rogue deck.

Season calendar

One season is four weeks. The events are designed to feed each other instead of competing for the same session:

Week 1Week 2Week 3Week 4Battle passLogin trackWeekly rule modifierWeekly booster rotationWeekend Draft cupSpecial event + reward track (2 weekends)Event bundle sale
  • Weekly rule modifiers change a few rules or card effects for one week, so players are encouraged to try new deck builds. They reuse existing systems, so they're cheap to produce. Examples: half-price Rogue cards, every player starts with one Legendary, or same-rank cards also score, etc.
    • Effects are only boosted, never nerfed. A temporary buff feels like a bonus; a temporary nerf feels like losing something you own.
    • Only a few effects change per week, so updating a deck takes minutes, not an evening.
    • Changes start every Monday and stay fixed for the whole week. The next week's changes are announced over the weekend, so players can prepare their decks in advance.
    • They apply to all modes, including ranked.
    • They give an early signal for planned balance changes. They're not how permanent changes are decided, though: too much changes at once (several boosts, the whole player base, the time in the season) to measure one effect cleanly, so permanent changes are confirmed with a controlled A/B test first.
  • The weekly booster pack runs all month and is themed to that week's boosted effects: in a week where Score Multiplier is boosted, the pack has a higher chance of multiplier effects. The weekly challenges point the same way.
  • Weekend Draft cups reward tokens. The special event runs for 9 days across two weekends, so players who mostly play on weekends don't miss it, and its reward track hands out cosmetic and art-upgrade tokens that feed the collection milestones.
  • The event bundle sale runs on the event's first weekend, when engagement is highest. Seasonal sales (Christmas, anniversary) add better gem rates.

Retention systems

Onboarding: the first hours

  • New players get a complete starter deck. It isn't the best deck, but it's good enough, so the first matches are fun and winnable.
  • One-time quests pay out generously and introduce each system: open a pack, craft a card, play Draft, change a card back, visit the shop. The shop quest shows both payment options, gold and gems, without forcing a purchase.
  • The aim is an early run of fun matches and good cards. Players who enjoy their first hours and feel they own something are more likely to come back on day 2.

Daily quests and weekly challenges

  • More quests than required: 5 daily quests (3 needed) and 7 weekly challenges (5 needed). Players pick what fits how they like to play. Finishing all of them gives a small bonus.
  • One weekly system: each weekly challenge pays gold and battle pass XP, and finishing the week adds a booster pack. Progress is rewarded per challenge, so it's never "all or nothing".
  • Rerolls: one free reroll per category, after that a small fee that rises with each reroll. A reroll never brings back a quest that was already offered.
  • One daily quest can target a known design problem. "Win a match with 3 Common Rogue effects" directly tests the underbought-Commons finding from the playtest logs.

Logging in and coming back

  • Daily free pack for a very easy objective (play one match).
  • Monthly login track with no streak reset: rewards grow with the number of days logged in, so missing a day never wipes out progress.
  • Comeback rewards: after 7 days away, a welcome-back mission plus 1 pack. After 30 days, a short mission chain plus 3 packs.
  • Account milestones with slowly growing rewards, including loyalty cosmetics that can't be bought.

Battle pass

TierPriceContents
Premium$9.99About $30 of value: gems, packs, card fragments, cosmetic tokens (choose your own cosmetic) and season-specific cosmetics
Epic$19.99Premium + instant 15-level jump + exclusive cosmetic
Legendary$29.99Premium + instant 30-level jump + exclusive cosmetic
  • 50 levels, 1,000 XP each. Every level costs the same. The free track also holds the rewards that would otherwise sit in monthly quests (packs, gold and an art-upgrade token), so there's one season-long goal instead of two.
  • Rewards don't stop at level 50. Engaged and Hardcore players often finish within days, so every level beyond 50 keeps paying small bonuses (gold, fragments, the occasional pack or token). Each one is small, but they add up over the season, so continued play always pays off.
  • Weekly challenges are the main XP source: 5 challenges count per week, 2,000 XP each (10,000 XP per week, plus gold), doable in about 5 matches, e.g. "Play 3 matches", "Win a Draft match" or "Play under this week's modifier". Unfinished challenges carry over until the season ends, so a missed week can be caught up.
  • XP per match comes on top: PvP win 500, PvP loss 250 (surrendering earns nothing), plus 10 XP per active minute. PvE matches earn less XP depending on difficulty. An average 15-minute match is worth about 525 XP (half wins, half losses: (500 + 250) ÷ 2 = 375, plus 15 minutes × 10 XP = 150).
  • Only bought with money, not gems. Upgrading to a higher tier costs only the difference.
  • Season leaderboard ranked by earned battle pass XP only. Level skips from the Epic and Legendary tiers don't count, so the top spots can't be bought. It comes with achievements for earned-XP milestones (e.g. the XP of level 100 and 150, also without skips) and a cosmetic for the top 1% of the season.

XP per season = weekly challenges (4 weeks × 10,000 XP, Casual completes 75%) + matches × 525, e.g. Regular: 40,000 + 54 × 525 = 68,350. Level reached = XP ÷ 1,000. Finish day = XP still needed ÷ XP per season × 28 days, assuming XP is earned evenly over the season: level 50 needs 50,000 XP, Epic 35,000 (15 levels skipped), Legendary 20,000 (30 skipped), e.g. Regular: 50,000 ÷ 68,350 × 28 ≈ day 20.

Why weekly challenges: if XP came only from matches, any setting that lets a Casual player finish would let everyone else finish within days. Weekly challenges give every player the same fixed amount of XP for a few sessions per week, so Regular players finish the $9.99 pass around day 20 while Casual players get most of the way: a Casual player who completes about three quarters of the challenges reaches level 40, and the Epic tier gets them to the end. Casual players who buy a pass are likely to complete more challenges than the average, so many will finish it anyway. Epic and Legendary save real time, and Engaged players keep going with repeating rewards, the leaderboard and level milestones. If paying players can't finish what they bought, they're unlikely to buy the next pass.

Guardrails

  • All drop rates are shown in the shop, and rare pulls are highlighted during pack opening.
  • Every card and effect can be earned for free. Random and Draft ignore the collection entirely.
  • Crafted cards stay Default art, so crafting never replaces packs.
  • Nerfed cards can be dismantled for their full craft cost.
  • A pity system guarantees a Legendary within a set number of packs.

What the model changed

The economy model isn't just a calculator for the final numbers. Several design decisions changed because the model showed a problem:

1. Battle pass XP: from matches only to weekly challenges

The first version gave XP only for matches. Whatever XP per level I chose, the model showed the same problem: a Casual player plays about 20 matches a season and a Regular player about 54, so any setting that lets Casual finish also lets everyone else finish within days.

Row 1: level 50 needs 50 × 500 = 25,000 XP. Casual: 10,500 ÷ 500 = level 21. Regular: 25,000 ÷ 28,350 × 28 ≈ day 25 · Engaged ≈ day 9 · Hardcore ≈ day 4.
Row 2: 210 XP per level is chosen so level 50 needs 10,500 XP, exactly Casual's match XP, so Casual finishes on day 28. Regular: 10,500 ÷ 28,350 × 28 ≈ day 10 · Engaged ≈ day 4 · Hardcore ≈ day 2.
Row 3: level 50 needs 50,000 XP. Weekly challenges add 4 × 10,000 = 40,000 XP (Casual completes 75%: 30,000). Casual: 30,000 + 10,500 = 40,500 XP = level 40; Epic skips 15 levels, so 35,000 ÷ 40,500 × 28 ≈ day 24. Regular: 50,000 ÷ 68,350 × 28 ≈ day 20 · Engaged (121,900 XP) ≈ day 11 · Hardcore (229,000 XP) ≈ day 6.

Decision: weekly challenges became the main XP source. They give every player the same fixed XP for a few sessions a week, which narrows the gap between Casual and Regular players, while Epic and Legendary still save real time.

2. Monthly quests removed

With weekly challenges driving the battle pass, monthly quests became a second month-long goal with the same job. Decision: removed them and moved their rewards into the free battle pass track. The model confirmed the free pack budget stays within the 1–2 packs per active day target for every profile.

3. Weekly challenge completion linked across the model

The battle pass sheet originally assumed every player completes all weekly challenges, while the economy sheet assumed Casual players complete only a quarter. Linking both to the same input showed that a Casual player completing a quarter of the challenges would only reach level 20 (a quarter of the challenges is 10,000 XP, plus 10,500 XP from 20 matches = 20,500 XP). Decision: Casual players are modeled at 75% completion, and the Epic tier is positioned as their route to finishing the pass.
